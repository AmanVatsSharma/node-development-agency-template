If customers find you by searching "near me" or by typing a service and a city, your Google Business Profile and a handful of local signals decide whether you appear in the map results or not. Local SEO is not mysterious: it is mostly about accurate information, consistent details across the web, genuine reviews and a website that proves you serve the area. This checklist walks through each part in the order that gives the best return.

## How local rankings work

Google describes local results as being based mainly on three factors: **relevance** (how well your profile matches what the person searched), **distance** (how far you are from the location in the search, or from the searcher), and **prominence** (how well known and trusted your business appears, including reviews, links and mentions across the web).

You cannot change distance, but you can improve the other two. That is where this checklist focuses. Rankings also differ by device, location and personalisation, so avoid judging results from one phone in one place.

## Step 1: Claim and verify your Google Business Profile

Everything else builds on this. Search for your business on Google Maps and claim the listing if one exists, or create a new one. Then verify it using the method Google offers (video, phone, email or postcard depending on your category and location). An unverified profile will not perform.

Use the business name exactly as it appears on your signboard, invoices and website. Adding keywords or locations to the name is against Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.

## Step 2: Complete every field that applies

| Field | What to do |
|---|---|
| Primary category | Choose the most specific category that matches your main service. This is one of the strongest relevance signals |
| Additional categories | Add only categories that genuinely describe what you do |
| Address or service area | Use the full address if customers visit you. If you travel to customers, set service areas and hide the address as Google allows |
| Hours | Keep them accurate, including special and festival hours |
| Phone | Use a number you actually answer, ideally the same one shown on your website |
| Website | Link to the most relevant page, usually the home page or a location page |
| Services or products | List real services with short descriptions and, where it makes sense, prices |
| Description | Write a natural summary within the character limit. Do not stuff keywords |
| Attributes | Mark accessibility, payment methods, appointment options and so on where offered |
| Photos and video | Add real photos of the storefront, interior, team and work. Fresh photos help customers trust you |

## Step 3: Keep your name, address and phone number consistent

Search engines cross-check your business details across the web. Inconsistent names, old addresses and multiple phone numbers weaken trust and confuse customers.

- Pick one canonical format for your name, address and phone number (often called NAP) and use it everywhere.
- List the business on relevant directories. In India, that often includes major local directories and, depending on your sector, industry or trade portals. Prefer directories that real customers use over long lists of low-quality sites.
- Find and merge duplicate listings. Duplicates split reviews and signals.
- If you move or change numbers, update every listing, not just the main one.

## Step 4: Build a steady flow of genuine reviews

Reviews influence both ranking and the decision to call or visit.

- **Ask at the right moment.** Send the review link right after a good experience, by WhatsApp, SMS or a printed QR code at the counter.
- **Respond to every review**, positive or negative, politely and specifically. Replying to criticism shows future customers how you handle problems.
- **Never buy reviews or offer rewards in exchange for positive ones**, and do not filter who you ask based on how happy you think they are. Google's policies prohibit fake and incentivised reviews, and getting caught can remove reviews or suspend a profile.
- **Aim for steady, recent reviews** instead of a burst followed by silence.

## Step 5: Make your website prove you are local

Your profile points customers to your site, so the site should confirm what Google already knows.

- Put your full NAP in a consistent place, such as the footer and contact page.
- If you serve several cities or have several branches, create a distinct page for each with unique content about that location. Avoid near-duplicate pages that only swap the city name.
- Add a map embed and clear directions on the contact or location page.
- Add `LocalBusiness` structured data with accurate details. See our [free SEO audit](/pages/seo-audit) if you would like a review of how your site presents these signals.
- Make sure pages load quickly on mobile. Most local searches happen on phones.
- Use the language your customers use. Depending on your market, that may include Hindi or regional-language terms alongside English.

## Step 6: Publish content that matches local intent

Pages and posts about the problems people in your area actually have help you appear for more searches. Examples: a dentist writing about "root canal cost in [city]", a CA firm explaining local GST filing deadlines for small shops, a school describing admission steps for the coming session. Keep content accurate, specific and useful. Updating your Google Business Profile with posts, offers and events gives customers fresh reasons to engage.

## Step 7: Track what matters

Without measurement you are guessing.

- **Google Business Profile performance** shows searches, calls, direction requests and website clicks.
- **Use a tagged link** on the profile's website field so visits from your profile appear as their own source in analytics.
- **Track calls.** A call tracking number or call reporting shows which channel produces enquiries.
- **Check Google Search Console** for the search terms that bring people to your site, and watch for changes after updates.
- **Check rankings carefully.** Use a tool that can simulate different locations, or check from the area you want to serve, because results vary by position.

## Common mistakes

- Keyword-stuffing the business name or description.
- Using a virtual office or a co-working address as if it were a staffed location.
- Leaving old phone numbers and addresses on directories.
- Creating separate profiles for the same location.
- Ignoring reviews, especially negative ones.
- Treating the profile as one-time setup instead of a living page.
- Running ads and SEO without comparing them. Our guide to [SEO vs Google Ads for Indian businesses](/pages/blog/seo-vs-google-ads-which-better-india) helps you decide how to split your effort.

## FAQ

### How long does local SEO take to work?

Improvements to a profile can show up within weeks, but meaningful gains in competitive categories usually take a few months of steady work. Expect progress to be gradual.

### Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

You can list a business with only a profile, but a good website gives Google and customers more proof of what you do and where. It also lets you rank for a wider range of searches.

### Can I run local SEO for several branches?

Yes. Give each branch its own profile (if it is a real, staffed location) and its own website page, and keep every branch's details consistent.

### Are paid ads better than local SEO?

They solve different problems. Ads give immediate visibility while you pay; local SEO builds a lasting presence. Many businesses use both. See our [Google Ads management](/pages/google-ads-management) service if you want paid help alongside SEO.

### What if my profile gets suspended?

Read the reason in your account, correct the issue (often the business name, address or category), and submit a reinstatement request with accurate proof of your business.
