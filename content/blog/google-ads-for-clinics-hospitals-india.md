Google Ads can fill appointment slots for clinics and hospitals quickly, because people search for a doctor or a treatment at exactly the moment they need one. But healthcare is one of the most regulated areas of advertising. Google restricts certain health-related ads, Indian law and professional rules limit what medical practitioners and hospitals can claim, and patient privacy needs extra care. This guide covers how to approach it responsibly: the rules to check, how to structure campaigns, what the landing page must do, how to set a budget, and how to measure appointments.

**This article is general information, not legal advice.** Advertising and professional rules change. Confirm the current requirements with your legal or compliance advisor and read Google's current policies before you launch.

## Why healthcare ads are different

Three layers of rules apply at once.

**1. Google's own advertising policies.** Google has policies for healthcare and medicines, and for personalised advertising. Among other things, certain products and services are restricted or prohibited, some require certification or are limited by country, and ads that make misleading or unsubstantiated health claims are disapproved. Google also treats personal health as a sensitive category, which limits how you can use audience and remarketing features. Read the current "healthcare and medicines" and "personalised advertising" policies in Google Ads Help before building anything.

**2. Indian advertising and professional rules.** Several rules affect what you may say, for example:

- the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954, which restricts advertising of certain remedies and claims;
- the professional conduct rules for doctors, set by the National Medical Commission, which limit how practitioners can advertise;
- the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) guidelines, which include rules for healthcare advertising and for testimonials and claims.

**3. Data protection.** If you collect names, phone numbers or health details through forms and calls, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 expects lawful purpose, consent and data minimisation. Do not send health information into advertising or analytics tools.

Treat these as the starting point of a compliance review, not the whole of it.

## Start with Search campaigns

Search campaigns are the most controlled and predictable way to begin, because you choose the queries and the ad copy. Other campaign types give you less control over where ads show, and some features are limited for sensitive categories. Start with Search, learn what converts, and expand carefully.

### Structure the account around services and locations

- One campaign per major service line (for example dental, dermatology, orthopaedics, IVF) or per branch, so budgets and reporting stay clear.
- Ad groups built around a single treatment or intent, such as "knee replacement" or "skin specialist".
- Location targeting that matches your catchment area. Use a radius or named areas around your branch. For hospitals that draw patients from a wider region, add the cities you actually serve.
- Ad schedules that match when you can answer the phone and book appointments.

## Keyword themes that work

| Theme | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Service plus location | "orthopaedic doctor in [area]", "dentist near me" | High intent; usually your core |
| Treatment or procedure | "root canal treatment", "cataract surgery" | Good for educational and booking pages |
| Specialist and clinic | "skin specialist", "child specialist" | Match the page to the specialty |
| Emergency and urgent | "24 hour clinic near me" | Only if you genuinely offer it and can answer |
| Branded | Your clinic and doctor names | Cheap and protects your name from competitors |

Add negative keywords to avoid wasted clicks, such as jobs, courses, salary, free, home remedy and self-treatment phrases. Review the search terms report weekly at first and add new negatives as you find them.

Be careful with symptom-based queries. They may attract people looking for advice rather than care, and some health queries are restricted by Google. Test small and watch both policy messages and lead quality.

## Write ads that are accurate and compliant

- Describe services and facilities factually: specialities, timings, location, languages, insurance acceptance if true.
- Do not promise outcomes, cures or guaranteed results, and avoid exaggerated claims or comparisons you cannot prove.
- Be cautious with testimonials, before-and-after images and awards. Check ASCI guidance and Google's policy first.
- Show doctor qualifications and registration details accurately where required.
- Keep the ad, the keyword and the landing page consistent.

## What the landing page must do

Google checks the landing page as part of ad review, and patients decide on trust within seconds.

- **Match the ad.** If the ad says "knee replacement consultation", the page is about that, not a generic home page.
- **Show who and where.** Doctor names and qualifications, branch address with a map, opening hours and a phone number that connects.
- **Make booking easy.** A short appointment form, click-to-call and a WhatsApp option, all working on mobile. See our [website and landing page work](/pages/web-development) if yours is slow or hard to use.
- **Ask for consent.** Tell people how you will use their details and link to a privacy policy.
- **Avoid collecting more than you need.** Do not ask for detailed medical history in a first-contact form.
- **Be fast and secure.** Use HTTPS and keep the page quick on mobile networks.
- **Stay compliant.** No unverifiable claims, no misleading before-and-after content.

## How to set a budget

Work backwards from appointments, not from a round number.

1. Decide how many new appointments you want per month.
2. Estimate the share of enquiries that become booked appointments, and of booked appointments that turn up.
3. Estimate the cost per click for your main keywords using Google's Keyword Planner, then adjust after real data.
4. Divide by your expected click-to-enquiry rate.

## Track what matters: appointments, not clicks

- **Count calls.** Use call assets and call tracking, and measure calls above a minimum length.
- **Count forms and WhatsApp clicks** as conversions.
- **Track booked and completed appointments**, not just enquiries. Where you can do so within privacy rules, import appointment outcomes back into Google Ads so bidding can learn which clicks lead to real patients.
- **Protect privacy.** Do not pass health details, names or phone numbers in page addresses, event names or conversion values. Configure analytics and tags so that no sensitive information leaves your system.
- **Review weekly:** search terms, cost per appointment, call answer rate and drop-off points.

## Common disapprovals and how to fix them

| Problem | Typical cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Healthcare and medicines policy | Restricted treatment, product or claim | Read the policy detail, remove or rephrase the restricted content, and appeal only after it complies |
| Misleading or unsubstantiated claims | "Best", "guaranteed cure", "100% success" | Replace with factual descriptions |
| Destination mismatch or not working | Page differs from ad, broken page, slow site | Fix the page and make the ad match it |
| Personalised advertising restrictions | Audiences or remarketing based on health conditions | Remove those audiences and use contextual targeting instead |
| Editorial issues | Capitalisation, punctuation, symbols | Edit the ad text as indicated |

## FAQ

### Can hospitals and clinics advertise on Google in India?

Generally yes for legitimate services, within Google's policies and Indian rules. Restricted categories, such as certain medicines and unproven treatments, are limited or prohibited. Check current policy before launch.

### How much should a clinic spend on Google Ads?

There is no single number. Work back from the appointments you want and your expected click cost and conversion rates, as in the example above, then adjust after a month of data.

### Should I run remarketing for patients who visited my site?

Be careful. Google restricts remarketing built around sensitive categories such as personal health. Review the current personalised advertising policy before you build audiences, and avoid targeting based on specific conditions.

### Are Performance Max or Display campaigns suitable?

They can work in some cases, but they give you less control over placements and messaging. Most healthcare advertisers start with Search, prove the economics and then test other formats cautiously.

### What should the tracking setup include?

Calls, form submissions and WhatsApp clicks as conversions, with appointment outcomes imported where privacy rules allow. Make sure no personal or health data is sent to advertising or analytics tools.
